# Frozen Bottle SEO Growth Case Study

> **Case study period:** May 2024 – May 2025  
> **Industry:** QSR / Food & Beverage  
> **Source:** [Original Behance case study](https://www.behance.net/gallery/256639559/How-we-scaled-SEO-Traffic-46-in-12-Months)  
> **Project material:** Frozen Bottle SEO Case Study (16-slide PDF supplied for this analysis)

## Overview

This case study describes an SEO growth system for Frozen Bottle designed to turn existing marketing demand and search behaviour into a larger, more durable organic acquisition channel. The presentation reports that monthly organic visits increased from **32,039 to 147,452** between May 2024 and May 2025, approximately **4.6× growth**.

The approach connected four workstreams:

1. **Campaign-led SEO** — use promotions and campaign activity as signals for search demand.
2. **Product SEO** — create transactional and navigational entry points for product launches, offers, and brand searches.
3. **Programmatic SEO** — scale relevant city-focused and long-tail content.
4. **Content velocity** — publish new content, observe search and campaign signals, and refresh existing articles continuously.

The central idea was to move from a content calendar to a demand-response system: campaign activity informs search opportunities, search intent informs content, and performance signals inform the next action.

### Interpreting the growth

The increase from 32,039 to 147,452 monthly organic visits is approximately **4.60×** the starting level, equivalent to an increase of about **360%** over the baseline. The source labels branded search growth as +195% from 24,399 to 72,095.

These are reported before/after figures. The presentation does not provide enough underlying analytics exports, attribution details, or a controlled comparison to isolate how much growth was caused by each tactic. Treat the results as case-study-reported outcomes, not independently verified causal estimates.

## Starting point and opportunity

The presentation identifies four opportunity areas at the beginning of the period:

- **Campaigns:** capture emerging demand created by promotions and social activity.
- **Products:** cover transactional and navigational search intent.
- **Content:** build consistent coverage for relevant searches.
- **Programmatic pages:** expand local and long-tail discovery.

The operating principle was:

**Demand → Intent → Coverage → Acquisition**

Campaigns and products provide demand signals; search terms and user behaviour help define intent; content, product destinations, and city pages provide coverage; organic visits and leads are the intended acquisition outcomes.

## Strategy and execution

### 1. Campaign-led SEO

The presentation describes a workflow that links campaign activity to search behaviour:

1. Run a promotion or social campaign.
2. Observe the searches people make around the offer or brand.
3. Create or improve a relevant page that answers that intent.
4. Capture ongoing organic demand after the campaign moment.

Examples shown in the presentation include searches related to a “Frozen Bottle 99 offer,” “Frozen Bottle offer,” “Frozen Bottle near me,” and “Frozen Bottle menu.” These are examples from the source, not evidence that every query received a dedicated page.

**Strategic takeaway:** campaigns can generate useful SEO research signals; campaign planning and search-intent mapping should inform each other.

### 2. Product SEO

Product launches were treated as opportunities to create new organic entry points rather than only catalogue updates.

The source outlines this sequence:

- Identify a new product, launch, or listing.
- Map transactional and navigational keyword opportunities.
- Create or select a relevant landing-page destination.
- Improve organic discovery for brand, product, and offer-related searches.

**Strategic takeaway:** launch pages should reflect how people search for a product, brand, or offer, with a clear destination that matches the query.

### 3. Programmatic SEO

The presentation reports **500+ programmatic articles and city-focused pages**, supported by a repeatable page model based on a template plus location or intent.

It also reports **10–12 organic leads per day from other cities**.

A scalable implementation should ensure each page offers useful, distinct information for its target location or intent. Templates should support consistency, but should not create large volumes of repetitive or low-value pages.

**Strategic takeaway:** programmatic SEO is most useful when there is a real set of distinct search needs and a reliable way to provide relevant content for each one.

### 4. Content velocity and refreshes

The source reports the following cadence:

- Publish **2 new blog posts per week**.
- Refresh **1 older article per week**.
- Observe campaign and search signals.
- Expand topics where demand appears.
- Use the results to decide what to publish, refresh, or expand next.

This makes content a feedback loop rather than a one-way publishing schedule.

**Strategic takeaway:** publishing volume is only one part of the system; observation and refresh work help the programme adapt to changing demand.

### 5. Search visibility beyond conventional listings

The presentation describes visibility across four surfaces:

- AI Overviews
- People Also Ask
- Featured snippets
- Branded search

It reports a **3–4× increase in total search visibility within one year**. The supplied deck does not include a detailed definition of this visibility metric or the underlying measurement export, so the figure should be attributed to the source.

## The operating model

The source's recurring loop can be represented as:

**Campaigns / product launches → Search signals → Intent mapping → Content or landing-page coverage → Organic discovery and leads → Performance review → Next action**

This connects marketing activity to SEO execution and creates a repeatable way to decide what to build or improve next.

## What can be replicated

The following are practical lessons inferred from the source strategy, not claims that every item was explicitly implemented in the project:

1. **Create a shared campaign-to-search workflow.** Include SEO research in campaign and launch planning, then review the queries that emerge.
2. **Map each page to a clear intent.** Separate informational, transactional, navigational, and local needs instead of targeting every query with the same page type.
3. **Build scalable templates with quality controls.** Validate location relevance, uniqueness, internal links, indexability, and usefulness before expanding page counts.
4. **Protect time for refreshes.** A steady update cadence can improve existing assets while new content expands coverage.
5. **Measure outcomes at multiple levels.** Track organic visits, branded demand, rankings, search visibility, leads, and downstream business outcomes where available.
6. **Keep reporting definitions consistent.** Record data sources, date ranges, metric definitions, and any seasonality or campaign changes before drawing conclusions.

## Measurement and evidence limitations

The supplied presentation communicates headline outcomes and a high-level strategy, but does not include the underlying exports or implementation detail needed to independently validate every claim.

For a more rigorous performance analysis, add supporting evidence where available:

- Google Analytics or equivalent organic landing-page and session reports.
- Google Search Console clicks, impressions, queries, pages, and date comparisons.
- A documented definition and source for “branded searches” and “search visibility.”
- A list or sample of programmatic URLs, with indexation and quality checks.
- Lead tracking and attribution details for the reported 10–12 daily leads.
- Notes on seasonality, campaigns, website changes, and other factors that may have influenced results.

## Conclusion

The strongest idea in this case study is the operating system behind the tactics: use real demand signals to shape search coverage, scale relevant pages where appropriate, and continuously improve content based on what the audience searches for.

The source reports growth from **32,039 to 147,452 monthly organic visits** over May 2024–May 2025. The reported outcomes are promising, while a full causal assessment would require the underlying analytics and attribution data.
